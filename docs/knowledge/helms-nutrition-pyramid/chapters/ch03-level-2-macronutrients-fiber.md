# Level 2: Macronutrients & Fiber

## Core Idea
Distribute Level 1's calories into **protein by bodyweight, fat and carbs by percentage**: protein's job is structural (scales with mass), fat/carbs' job is energy (scales with the calorie budget). Dieting shifts every number — more protein, less fat — because a cut is a zero-sum budget ("rob Peter to pay Paul").

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The mixed prescription method**: protein in g/lb (or g/kg); fat as % of calories; carbohydrate = everything left. Energy densities: **protein 4, carb 4, fat ~9 kcal/g** (alcohol 7, handled separately in Behavior).
- **Macronutrient recommendation table**:

| Phase | Protein | Fat | Carbs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cutting** | **1.1–1.3 g/lb** (2.3–2.8 g/kg) | **15–25%** of calories | remainder |
| **gaining** | **0.8–1.0 g/lb** (1.8–2.2 g/kg) | **20–30%** of calories | remainder |

- **Why protein rises while dieting**: restriction forces protein toward energy (less fat/carb available, falling glycogen/fat stores); lean dieting lifters lose muscle even training hard. Direct moderate-vs-high comparisons are imperfect, but 1.1–1.3 g/lb is certainly not harmful, is more satiating (spontaneously cuts intake), and improved mood/stress/dissatisfaction in Helms' own trial (1.3 vs 0.72 g/lb: same body comp, better subjective outcomes).
- **Why protein falls when gaining**: anabolic benefits of protein don't extend past ~0.8 g/lb in surplus; surplus energy spares protein, and excessive protein crowds out carbs/fat or kills appetite in a hard-gaining phase.
- **Floor macros (protects small eaters)**: minimum **0.25 g/lb (~0.5 g/kg) fat** and **0.5 g/lb (~1 g/kg) carbohydrate** — fat is essential; too little carb sabotages training (and training is weapon #1 for muscle retention; protein is #3 after rate of loss).
- **Insulin-resistance branch (~20% of people, 35%+ fat / 0.5–1.5 g/lb carbs)**: predictors — age (independent effects even in trained people), family history of diabetes, **PCOS**, **oligomenorrhea** (>35-day cycles; higher androgens, over-represented in strength sports, may even aid performance). Evidence favors higher-fat/higher-protein/lower-carb diets *for the insulin resistant*; for everyone else the standard split works. Keto itself isn't magic — you burn more fat because you eat more fat.
- **The 40/20 self-trial (off-season)**: hold calories and protein constant; run one month at **40% fat**, one month at **20% fat**, rating **mood, energy, and training quality 1–10 daily**; repeat both (4-month commitment). Higher average ratings on one arm = your long-term ratio. Gym *quality* ≠ gym *performance* — keep training identical and rate the perceived effort/mental state.
- **Fiber**: minimum **20 g/day (F), 25 g/day (M)** or blanket **10 g per 1,000 kcal** (better for keto/low-cal); maximum **20% of carbohydrate intake** — excess impairs mineral absorption and GI function and causes bloat. Count fiber as carbohydrate (fermentation calories are unknowable; simplicity wins). Satiety bonus: fiber "counts" as carbs but delivers fewer calories.

## Key Concepts
- **Who the defaults serve**: non-overweight, lifting regularly, under ~60 — "about 80% of people." Aging independently reduces carb tolerance and protein's anabolic response while expenditure falls.
- Coach's-corner distribution: ~10–15% of clients run >30% fat; ~5% near-keto (80–120 g carbs or less).
- The pyramid is honest about its own blind spot: training matters more to muscle retention than any macro split (→ companion training book).

## Anti-patterns
- **Fixing a too-aggressive deficit with protein** — a moderate-protein lifter losing 0.5–1%/week beats a high-protein lifter losing 1.5–2%/week. Level 1 outranks Level 2.
- **Setting carbs before fat** — fat is essential, carbs aren't; set fat, give carbs the remainder.
- **Panic at sub-1.0 g/lb protein in a surplus** — the evidence caps anabolic benefit ≈0.8 g/lb.
- **Fiber maximalism** ("clean" oats-and-vegetables diets) — beyond ~20% of carbs it backfires.

## Worked Example
**Gaining (200 lb male, maintenance 3,000, +200 kcal → 3,200)**: protein 1 g/lb = 200 g = 800 kcal; fat 25% = 800 kcal ≈ 89 g; carbs = 3,200 − 1,600 = 1,600 kcal = **400 g**. → **200 P / 90 F / 400 C**.

**Cutting (same lifter, 2,500 kcal after −500)**: protein 1.1–1.2 g/lb ≈ 225 g = 900 kcal; fat 20% = 500 kcal ≈ 56 g (above the 50 g floor); carbs = 1,100 kcal = **275 g**. → **225 P / 55 F / 275 C** — enough carbs to train, enough fat to stay sane, enough protein to protect lean mass.

## Key Takeaways
1. Protein g/lb (1.1–1.3 cutting, 0.8–1.0 gaining); fat 15–25% cutting / 20–30% gaining; carbs take the rest.
2. Never breach the floors: 0.25 g/lb fat, 0.5 g/lb carbs.
3. Insulin-resistance signs (age, family history, PCOS, oligomenorrhea) → consider the higher-fat branch, verified by the 40/20 month-trial.
4. Fiber 10 g/1,000 kcal (or 20/25 g minimum), capped at 20% of carbs, counted as carbs.
