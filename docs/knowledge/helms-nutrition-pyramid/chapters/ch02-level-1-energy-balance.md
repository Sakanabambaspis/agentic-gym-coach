# Level 1: Energy Balance

## Core Idea
The most important level: set calories from your **target rate of bodyweight change**, derived from your maintenance calories. Find maintenance by *tracking* (best) or by *equation* (fallback); lose 0.5–1.0%/week; gain by training age. Getting Level 1 right is "about 70–80% of the battle."

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Maintenance by tracking (preferred)**: weigh/track food and daily bodyweight for **2 weeks**. Average the 14 calorie days; split weigh-ins into two 7-day averages; convert weight change to an energy surplus/deficit via the **3500-calorie rule** (1 lb adipose ≈ 3500 kcal), then back out maintenance. Works because short-term, un-manipulated weight change is mostly fat + water, and 7-day averages kill water noise. Caveats: invalid for complete novices and lifters returning from layoff (rapid muscle change).
- **Maintenance by equation (fallback)**: bodyweight (lb) × **10** (or kg × 22) → baseline → × activity multiplier:
  - Sedentary + lifting 3–6×/wk: **1.3–1.6** · Lightly active: **1.5–1.8** · Active: **1.7–2.0** · Very active: **1.9–2.2**.
  - Example: 200 lb male → 2,000 baseline → 2,600–4,400 kcal maintenance. The width of that range is the argument for the 2-week tracking method.
- **NEAT (non-exercise activity thermogenesis)**: subconscious movement is a main regulator of expenditure — bodies adapt to defend "normal weight," and individuals vary enormously ("thrifty" metabolisms). Consequence: you may need more or fewer calories *than the math says* to hit a target rate; treat all numbers as starting points to adjust by weekly-average weight trends.
- **Rate of weight loss**: **0.5–1.0% of bodyweight per week** (200 lb male → 1–2 lb/week → subtract 500–1,000 kcal/day). Faster costs muscle and strength; someone eating modest protein at 0.5–1%/week retains more muscle than someone eating high protein at 1.5–2%/week — level priority matters.
- **Rate of weight gain (by training age)**:

| Level | Definition | Monthly gain |
|---|---|---|
| Beginner | progresses week-to-week | 1–1.5% bodyweight |
| Intermediate | progresses month-to-month | 0.5–1% |
| Advanced | progress visible over months/year | up to 0.5% |

  Surplus math (3500 × monthly target ÷ 30): beginner 180 lb male ~200–300 kcal/day (130 lb female ~150–225); intermediate ~100–200 (75–150); advanced ≤100 (≤75). A 12-week study: small surplus gained the **same muscle and strength with one-fifth the fat** of a +600 kcal group — classic bulking is dead. Advanced lifters: chase gym progress (reps/load), not scale weight; expect mini-cuts. Weekly translation: beginner ~¾ lb/week, intermediate ~½ lb/week, advanced ≈ maintenance.
- **Cardio for fat loss (sub-framework)**:
  - Energy cost ≈ **0.2 / 0.45 / 0.7 kcal per 10 min per lb bodyweight** above rest for light / moderate / vigorous cardio (200 lb male moderate: ~90 kcal/10 min).
  - **Interference**: moderate-intensity cardio is endurance training — dose-dependent interference with strength/hypertrophy (glycogen depletion, molecular signaling, eccentric/impact stress; cycling interferes less than incline walking). Low-intensity non-impact cardio is below the adaptive threshold (no interference, low burn). HIIT is time-efficient, less interfering (more similar to resistance training), carries a small post-exercise metabolic bump — but eccentric/impact modalities injure (sprinters: >2× hamstring injuries of distance runners).
  - **Cardio rules**: diet creates most of the deficit; total weekly cardio ≤ **half your lifting time**; low-impact, non-soreness modalities (cycling, rowing, swimming, elliptical, light complexes); ≤**2 HIIT sessions/week, ≤30 min each**; ≤**1 hour/week of moderate** (most interfering); rest low-intensity. Small women near the food-intake floor may need to max or slightly exceed this.

## Key Concepts
- **3500-calorie rule**: an imperfect but useful simplification — 1 lb adipose tissue ≈ 3500 kcal; valid for short un-manipulated windows.
- **Metabolic magic (tissue-energy accounting)**: 1 lb *pure* fat ≈ 4,270 kcal; 1 lb hydrated muscle ≈ only ~800–830 kcal deposited. So gaining muscle while losing fat in a small deficit is arithmetically possible (study: overweight sarcopenic elderly men gained 3.7 lb muscle, lost 2.4 lb fat, **gained** 1.3 lb weight on a ~65 kcal/day deficit); likewise small fat gain in a deficit if muscle is being lost (bedridden, low protein). Practical echoes: gaining "weight" on a diet break (glycogen + gut content) while getting leaner; slow recomp for advanced lifters even without enforced surplus. But recomp is *slow* — dedicated cut/gain phases remain the recommendation.
- **Muscle is metabolically costly**: building tissue raises expenditure — with NEAT, this explains weight gain smaller than predicted after calorie increases.
- Calories can vary day to day; the **average over time** vs maintenance is what drives the target rate.

## Anti-patterns
- **Classic dirty bulking** — lower strength-to-weight ratio, longer cuts, mostly extra fat.
- **Cardio as the primary deficit vehicle** (7 hrs/week moderate to "keep food") — interference and joint cost.
- **Reading single weigh-ins** as progress or failure.
- **Trusting the equation over the 2 weeks of data** you could have collected.

## Worked Example
**Two-week maintenance derivation**: 14-day average intake 2,100 kcal; week-1 average weight 142.1 lb, week-2 142.3 lb → gained 0.2 lb ≈ 700 kcal weekly surplus ≈ 100 kcal/day → maintenance ≈ 2,000 kcal. To cut 1 lb/week: eat ~1,500 kcal/day (or ~1,700 + ~200 kcal/day of cardio).

**Max cardio week for a 6-hour lifter** (≤3 h total): 2 × 30-min HIIT + 1 h moderate + 1 h low-intensity — that's the *ceiling*, not a target.

## Key Takeaways
1. Track 2 weeks to find maintenance; use the equation only when time-constrained.
2. Cut at 0.5–1.0%/week; bulk at 0.5–1.5%/month by training age — advanced gainers barely move the scale on purpose.
3. NEAT and adaptation mean all calorie numbers are hypotheses; weekly weight averages are the arbiter.
4. Diet drives the deficit; cardio is capped, low-impact, and mostly low-intensity.
5. Expect scale "magic" at diet-phase boundaries (glycogen/water); judge fat loss over weeks, not days.
