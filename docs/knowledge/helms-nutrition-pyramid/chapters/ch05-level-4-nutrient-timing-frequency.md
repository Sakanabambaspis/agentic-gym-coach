# Level 4: Nutrient Timing & Frequency

## Core Idea
Timing is the fourth-most-important dial: distribute calories across the *diet's timeline* (diet breaks), the *week* (refeeds), the *day* (meal frequency), and *around training* (peri-workout meals) — in that order of scale. None of it rescues a broken Level 1 or 2, and the leanness-dependent payoff curve means novices should mostly skip this chapter.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Diet breaks (periodization of the diet)**: a planned 1–3-week return to ~maintenance — "get your oil changed before the car breaks down." Who: anyone dieting **≥3 months** without a hard deadline behind schedule. Implementation: **+300–600 kcal on deficit days** (to ~maintenance) with **cardio cut to ~50%**; eat as much as possible without meaningful gain. Schedule example: 24-week prep → breaks at weeks 8, 16, 24 (peak week marries with carb-load). Outcomes — gain (glycogen/water/lean rebound), maintain, or even *lose* (cortisol drop releasing water; expenditure rising) — are all acceptable; you return with more food tolerance, less cardio, more muscle and sanity.
- **Metabolic adaptation (why breaks work)**: after a ≥10% weight loss, TDEE runs **~18% below** predicted on average (95% spread ~8–28%) — adaptive thermogenesis beyond the explained drop from moving less mass. Part of the adaptive component is reversible by raising calories; breaks also replenish glycogen, reduce fatigue, and restore training quality.
- **Single-day refeeds**: one day/week at **maintenance, driven by carbohydrate** (protein/fat held at diet-day values, carbs up to the maintenance number). The other 6 days must deepen to hold the weekly deficit — example: 1.5 lb/week loss = 5,250 kcal/week → 750/day on 7 dieting days vs **875/day on 6**. A 24-h refeed does *not* meaningfully reverse hormonal adaptation (48 h+ at maintenance likely needed — one day is simply one less day of mounting adaptation), but buys psychology, glycogen, and training energy. Use from the start of a diet, when adaptation is a non-issue.
- **Multi-day refeeds / carb cycling**: once below **~12% BF (men) / ~20% (women)**, upgrade to **2 consecutive refeed days** (deficit spread over 5 days) or **3 spread refeeds** (deficit over 4). Research basis: intermittent restriction has beaten continuous restriction for fat loss and lean-mass retention in head-to-heads. The leaner you get, the more frequent the refeeds.
- **Refeed placement**: only matters when low/high-day disparity is large (e.g. 3 refeeds → low days ~1,300 kcal below refeeds). Glycogen resynthesis takes up to 24 h → place refeeds the **day before** your highest-volume sessions if you train morning/afternoon; **same day** only if you train in the evening. Training-day vs off-day calorie cycling otherwise is unnecessary — a 90-min lifting session burns only ~200–400 kcal (a couple hundred more than resting), and adaptation runs on all days, so don't starve off days.
- **Weekend-flex restructure (gaining)**: if weekends routinely blow the surplus, drop weekdays to no more than **10% below maintenance** and bank the difference for the weekend (5 days at −200 ≈ +1,000 kcal to redistribute).
- **Meal frequency**: 3–6 meals/day for most; extremes hurt (≤2 meals → ravenous feasts, disordered large-meal training; >6 → constant food focus, unsatisfying micro-meals). Energy expenditure is identical from 2–7 meals; **consistency of pattern** matters more than the number (irregular timing slightly lowers expenditure and insulin sensitivity). Exceptions granted: IF-ers succeeding on 2 meals, high-frequency gainers needing 6–7 to physically get 5,000 kcal down.
- **Peri-workout nutrition**:
  - **Post-workout carbs**: mostly unnecessary for lifters — a 9–12 set body-part session depletes ≤~40% of that muscle's glycogen and you won't hit it again for days; full resynthesis occurs within 24 h even on moderate carbs. Protein spikes insulin as well as carbs do; timing studies mostly show no difference. (Exception: same-day HIIT + lifting.)
  - **Pre-workout carbs**: genuinely individual and inconsistent in the literature — experiment. Avoid too-much-too-close (GI upset, reactive hypoglycemia) and long gaps/missed habitual meals (early fatigue). While dieting, ~28 g easily-digestible carbs 1–2 h pre-workout improved muscular endurance over 8 weeks.
  - **Peri-workout protein**: total daily protein dominates; the "anabolic window" is a whole-day phenomenon (meta-analysis: ≤small hypertrophy edge for ≤1-h timing once 24-h protein is controlled). Pragmatic dose: **0.18–0.23 g/lb (0.4–0.5 g/kg) protein 1–2 h pre and post training**.
  - **Cutting**: normal mixed meal (protein ≈0.2 g/lb, **10–20% of daily carbs**, 10–15% of daily fat if reactive hypoglycemia-prone) 1–2 h before and after training.
  - **Gaining**: any meal with protein within ~2.5 h pre and within 2 h post suffices; shift food *away* from pre-training if GI distress from volume.
  - **≥2 h continuous hard training** (e.g., intervals after leg day): liquid nutrition 8–15 g protein + 30–60 g carbs split pre/intra (whey + Gatorade style). Explicitly *not* a 2-h weights session that's mostly rest.

## Anti-patterns
- **Timing obsession before fundamentals** — the leanness/experience payoff curve is nearly flat for novices.
- **Refeeds as binges** — calculated maintenance-carb days, not cheat days.
- **6 small unsatisfying meals** or **2 feast meals** when 3–4 would serve.
- **Starving off days / "carb backloading" logic** built on a training-day expenditure difference of a couple hundred kcal.

## Worked Example
**1.5 lb/week cut with 2 refeed days (lean, 3-refeed variant shown)**: weekly deficit 5,250 kcal → spread over 4 dieting days = ~1,310/day below maintenance; refeed days at maintenance via carbs. Train Mon/Wed/Fri afternoons → place refeeds Sun/Tue/Thu (the day before each session). Expect low days to feel big — that's the trade for 3 maintenance days.

## Key Takeaways
1. Diet ≥3 months → schedule 1–3-week maintenance breaks (+300–600 kcal, cardio halved).
2. Refeeds scale with leanness: 1/week → 2–3/week below ~12%/20% BF; drive them with carbs at maintenance.
3. Eat 3–6 meals at a *consistent* pattern; frequency itself is neutral.
4. Peri-workout: protein 0.18–0.23 g/lb within 1–2 h on both sides; carbs pre-workout only if dieting (10–20% of daily) or proven individually.
