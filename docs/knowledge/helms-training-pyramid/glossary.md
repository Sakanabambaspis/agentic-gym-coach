# Glossary

**Accumulation block** — A ~6-week mesocycle building volume (and thus muscle/work capacity) at moderate RPE before intensification (Ch 4, Ch 9)

**Adherence (Level 1)** — The foundation pyramid level: training must be Realistic, Enjoyable, Flexible (REF) (Ch 2)

**AMRAP** — As many reps as possible; used to test or estimate strength (estimate 1RM only from ~5RM-or-heavier AMRAPs) (Ch 4, Ch 9)

**Antagonist paired set (APS)** — Pairing opposite muscle groups (bench↔row, curls↔pushdowns) so one rests while the other works; time-efficient without costing performance (Ch 6)

**Autoregulation** — Adjusting days, load, deloads, or exercise choice by readiness/RPE instead of fixed plans (Ch 2, Ch 8)

**BFR (blood flow restriction)** — Cuff/wrap at ~7/10 tightness; 20–30% 1RM sets to failure give a hypertrophy stimulus while sparing painful joints (Ch 2, Ch 4)

**Block periodization** — Dividing training into short mesocycles (accumulation → intensification → realization) to preserve adaptations and peak multiple times per season (Ch 4)

**Deload** — A planned low-stress week (~half volume, similar loads, ~2 RPE lower) that dissipates fatigue so progress can continue (Ch 3, Ch 4)

**Double progression** — Adding reps to the top of a range before adding load; the isolation-exercise progression (Ch 4, Ch 8)

**Drop set** — Reaching failure then reducing load and continuing; time-efficient but hard to quantify (Ch 6)

**DUP (daily undulating periodization)** — Rotating rep range/goal (hypertrophy, power, strength) across days of the week (Ch 4)

**Eccentric muscle action** — The controlled lowering phase; you're 20–40% stronger eccentrically, which is why overloaded eccentrics work in labs but slow eccentrics don't in the gym (Ch 7)

**Exercise-induced muscle damage** — Normal fiber disruption from training; a byproduct of overload, not a goal; in excess it impairs growth (Ch 3, Ch 5)

**First Set RPE** — Programming load so the *first* set hits the target RPE; later sets use the same load (Ch 9)

**Fitness-Fatigue Model** — Performance = fitness − fatigue; fatigue masks fitness and dissipates faster, which is why deloads/tapers reveal progress (Ch 3)

**Frequency** — Sessions per week per muscle/movement; organizes volume and intensity; 2+/week per muscle as the baseline (Ch 3)

**Functional overreaching** — Planned overload that a deload converts into supra-baseline performance (Ch 3)

**Impulse** — Force × time; the physics frame for hypertrophy stimulus — tension has magnitude as well as duration (Ch 7)

**Intensity (of load / of effort)** — How heavy (%1RM, RM) vs how close to failure (RPE/RIR); the two meanings must be kept distinct (Ch 3)

**Interference effect** — Concurrent endurance work slows (not stops) strength/hypertrophy gains; separate hard cardio from lifting by 6+ hours or a day (Ch 2)

**Intro cycle** — A week at ~75% of planned volume and slightly lower RPE that acclimates you before a volume block (Ch 4)

**Linear periodization** — Volume decreases as intensity increases over time; for intermediates, run as weekly wave loading (Ch 4)

**Macrocycle / mesocycle / microcycle** — The season/year plan / a 3–6-week block / one training week (Ch 4)

**Mind-muscle connection** — Internal cueing that boosts target-muscle activation at light loads; effect vanishes ≥80% 1RM on compounds (Ch 5)

**Nonfunctional overreaching** — Unplanned overload that returns you only to baseline — "wheel-spinning" (Ch 3)

**Overtraining** — Extreme fatigue that prevents maintaining fitness; rare in lifting, needs long recovery, not a deload (Ch 3)

**Periodization** — The intentional manipulation of training variables over time; the wrapper around all pyramid levels (Ch 1, Ch 4)

**Power day / technique day** — Low-volume, submaximal (RPE 4–7) heavy practice on main lifts; builds skill and aids recovery (Ch 4, Ch 9)

**Primary/secondary muscles** — A movement's direct vs indirect contributions; count both 1:1 toward weekly sets but ensure direct work exists (Ch 3, Ch 5, Ch 8)

**Progression vs progressive overload** — Overload is provided by correct VIF; progression is the plan for continuing overload as you near your genetic limit (Ch 4)

**RIR (repetitions in reserve)** — Reps you could still have done at set end; the basis of the RPE scale (Ch 3)

**RPE (repetitions-in-reserve based)** — 10 = failure, 9 = 1 RIR, 8 = 2 RIR, 7 = 3 RIR; the effort dial that individualizes load prescriptions (Ch 3)

**Reactive deload** — Deciding deloads via a post-block checklist (2+ flags → deload; mandatory every third mesocycle) instead of a fixed schedule (Ch 4)

**Realization block** — The ~2-week taper/test phase where fitness is displayed as fatigue dissipates (Ch 4)

**Rest-pause set** — One load taken to failure repeatedly with 20–30 s rests to reach a rep target; accessory-only (Ch 6)

**Specificity (SAID)** — Adaptations match the demands imposed — movement, load, velocity, joint angle, and range of motion (Ch 3, Ch 5)

**Sticking point** — The region where a lift slows; pausing *at* it is flawed — address the weak link instead (Ch 5)

**Supramaximal eccentric** — Lowering more than your concentric max via spotter or machines; unproven benefits, high cost (Ch 7)

**Taper / peaking** — Reducing volume ~⅓–⅔ (intensity maintained) before competition to express fitness; powerlifters taper strength, bodybuilders taper to support the carb load (Ch 4)

**Technical failure** — Ending a set when form breaks rather than when the weight stops; the only failure mode permitted on big compounds (Ch 3, Ch 9)

**Training age** — Classified by *rate of improvement* (workout-to-workout / week-to-week / month-to-month), not years lifting or strength standards (Ch 4)

**Volume (sets)** — Hard sets per muscle/movement per week in an intensity zone; the book's volume currency (Ch 3)

**Volume load** — Sets × reps × load; distorted by high-rep sets, so not used for volume counting (Ch 3)

**Wave loading** — Intermediate progression: load up / reps down across 3 weeks (8→7→6), then restart heavier after an optional deload (Ch 4, Ch 9)

**Weak link in the chain** — The lagging muscle/joint limiting a compound lift; attacked with targeted accessories (Ch 5)
