# Quick Start Guide to Program Building

## Core Idea
The six levels compress into a five-step build: **(1)** pick a realistic number of training days and a split that hits every muscle/movement 2+×/week, **(2)** assign volume by training age and pair rep/RPE ranges to exercise classes, **(3)** assign the progression strategy your training age can support, **(4)** pick exercises and count their overlap, **(5–6)** add APS/time-savers — then layer autoregulation on top.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Step 1 — Days & split**: 2–6 days/week (novices can run 2; beyond novice, ~3+ is near-mandatory as 2-day volumes make sessions impractically long). For 90% of people: **3–5 days/week with 2–4×/week per muscle/movement**. Split options are indexed by frequency: strength splits by how often bench runs and how often squat+deadlift *combined* run (they share stimulus and lumbar stress); hypertrophy splits by muscle-group frequency (full body, upper/lower, legs/push/pull permutations). Frequency choice sets sets-per-session: 6 full-body days = short sessions; 2 = marathon sessions.
- **Step 2 — Volume by training age** (weekly sets per muscle/movement → frequency):

| Training age | Weekly sets | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Novice | 10–12 | 2–3× |
| Intermediate | 13–15 | 3–4× |
| Advanced | 16–20 | 3–5× |

  Spread volume so no session degrades; mismatch between volume and split means change one of them.
- **Step 2 — Rep/RPE pairing by exercise class** (more fatigue + technical demand → lower RPE cap and lower reps):

| Goal | Exercise class | Reps | RPE |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hypertrophy | Lower free-weight compound (squat, DL, RDL) | 3–8 | 5–8 |
| Hypertrophy | Lower machine compound (leg press, hack) | 6–12 | 6–9 |
| Hypertrophy | Lower isolation (extensions, curls, calves) | 8–20 | 7–10 |
| Hypertrophy | Upper free-weight compound (bench, OHP, row) | 3–12 | 6–9 |
| Hypertrophy | Upper machine compound (pulldown, cable row) | 6–15 | 6–10 |
| Hypertrophy | Upper isolation (curls, pushdowns, raises) | 8–20 | 7–10 |
| Strength | Main lift — specific strength | 1–5 | 7–10 |
| Strength | Main lift — volume (hypertrophy/work capacity) | 4–8 | 5–8 |
| Strength | Main lift — technique/"power" | 1–3 | 4–7 |
| Strength | Compound accessory (OHP, GM, BB row) | 4–8 | 6–9 |
| Strength | Machine/isolation accessory | 8–15 | 7–10 |

- **Step 3 — Progression strategy by training age and exercise type**:
  - **Linear load increases** — novice compounds (add smallest increment every repeat of the session).
  - **Linear periodization (wave loading)** — intermediate compounds; novices on isolation (load up, reps down across the week, restart heavier after deload/repeat).
  - **Block-periodized mesocycles** — advanced compounds (accumulation → intensification → taper & test or rinse-and-repeat).
  - **Double progression** — intermediate+ isolation (reps to top of range, then load up).
- **Step 4 — Exercise selection & counting**: count primary and secondary muscle contributions **1:1** toward a muscle's weekly sets (the research counted them equally), but don't let a muscle's volume come *only* from indirect work. Strength athletes count in three buckets (upper push / upper pull / lower) and verify **50–75% of total volume from the competition lifts**.
- **Autoregulation layer**:
  - **Days**: training 2–3×/week → float *training* days; 4+×/week → float *off* days.
  - **Load**: program %1RM **with an RPE range** (3×8 @ 70% @ 6–8 RPE); adjust load if set 1 lands outside the range. No tested 1RM on a lift → RPE alone. Novices: *track* RPE without programming by it for a few months until calibrated.
  - **Deloads**: post-block checklist after every mesocycle; mandatory by the third mesocycle without one.
  - **Exercise rotation**: hypertrophy — rotate compound patterns meso-to-meso (return to each every few mesos), isolations session-to-session (log loads so you can resume). Strength — rotate main-lift variants meso-to-meso *when far from competition* (use the competition lift as it approaches); accessories session-to-session.
- **Training in a cut**: brief/mild cuts need no changes. Long/aggressive cuts (weight class drop, contest prep): ① step **down one volume category** (advanced→intermediate logic) around the first third of the diet; ② deload **automatically after every mesocycle** instead of by checklist; ③ lean harder on autoregulation because performance gets noisy.

## Anti-patterns
- Running volumes that make 2-day splits into 3-hour sessions — quality collapses late in the session.
- Letting all of a muscle's volume come from secondary contributions.
- Programming by RPE before you're calibrated (novices), or by %1RM without an RPE escape hatch.
- Keeping checklist-deloads on a rigid schedule during an aggressive cut — switch to automatic.
- Ignoring the split↔volume mismatch: 16–20 sets at 2×/week means 8–10 sets per muscle per session.

## Worked Example
**Building the intermediate 4-day strength program (S, B, D, B split, 13–15 sets/bucket, 2×/week movement frequency):**
- **Day 1 Squat**: 5 squat-pattern sets (4–6 reps, wave loading) + 4 upper pull accessory sets (6–8, double progression)
- **Day 2 Bench**: 5 bench sets (4–6, wave) + 3 upper push accessory (6–8)
- **Day 3 Deadlift**: 5 deadlift sets (3–5, wave) + 3 lower accessory (6–8)
- **Day 4 Bench (volume)**: 4 bench sets (8–12, wave) + 3 upper push + 4 upper pull accessory (8–12, double progression)

**The audit that validates it**: Upper push = 9 bench + 6 accessories = 15 ✓; Lower = 10 squat/deadlift + 3 accessory = 13 ✓; Upper pull = 5 deadlift + 8 pulling = 13 ✓. Main lifts = 19 of 36 total sets ≈ 53% — inside the 50–75% specificity guideline ✓. Hypertrophy variant (Legs, Push, Pull, Full Body, 13–15 sets): ~9 sets/muscle across the three focus days + 4–8 on the full-body day; compounds wave-load in 8–12, isolations double-progress in 12–15 (6–8 on the full-body day).

## Key Takeaways
1. Sequence the build in level order — adherence decisions (days) constrain everything downstream.
2. Match weekly sets to training age, then *check* that your split's frequency can carry that volume.
3. Pair rep and RPE ranges to exercise class: heavy/technical lifts get capped RPE; machines and isolations carry the high-rep work.
4. Count secondary-muscle volume 1:1, but audit that each muscle gets direct work and that a strength program is ≥50% competition lifts.
5. Autoregulate days, load (RPE ranges), deloads, and rotation — and during hard cuts, drop a volume tier and deload automatically.
