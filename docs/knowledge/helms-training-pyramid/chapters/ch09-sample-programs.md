# Sample Programs: Six Blueprints (Novice/Intermediate/Advanced × Strength/Hypertrophy)

## Core Idea
The six programs are **learning tools, not prescriptions** — each is one possible iteration of the pyramid concepts, matched to goal and training age. The differences between levels are the *rate of progression attempted*, the *total volume*, and the *structure organizing that volume*; everything else stays constant. Volume for powerlifting programs is counted in three buckets (lower, upper push, upper pull); bodybuilding programs count per muscle group.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Warm-up template**: optional 5-min easy aerobic → dynamic full-body prep (10 leg swings each direction, 10 arm circles fwd/back, 10 cross-body arm slaps, 10 walking lunges with trunk rotation) → ramp-up sets on each lift. Static stretching *before* lifting can blunt performance — avoid it on muscles about to be trained, or: stretch <60 s without discomfort, foam-roll instead (increases ROM without force loss), or run the dynamic warm-up after stretching (negates the decrement). Ramp-ups: for 1–5-rep work sets → 50/60/70/80/90% × 5/5/4/3/2/1 (+bar); for 6+-rep work → 50%×8, 70%×4, 90%×2.
- **Novice Powerlifting (3- or 4-day)**: DUP week without formal periodization — Day 1 hypertrophy (squat + bench 3×8 @ 70%, vertical pull 4×10 @ 1st-set RPE 8), Day 2 "power"/technique (bench 3×3 @ 80%, deadlift 3×3 @ 85%) + vertical push and single-leg accessories, Day 3 strength (squat + bench 3×4 @ 85%) + horizontal pull. 3-day: accessories trail the main lifts; 4-day: Day 4 is accessories-only (shorter sessions, less fatigue bleed). Stats: 32 total sets — 12/11/12 per bucket, ~56% competition lifts, 67% of sets ≤6 reps.
- **Novice progression rules**: load by %1RM on main lifts (test a 3–5RM or true 1RM with spotters first; complete novices just practice with light loads). Accessories and no-% lifts: pick a load for the target reps at **first-set RPE 8** — if set 1 is right, later sets at the same load should all finish. Progress **+10 lb (~5 kg)/week on squat and deadlift, +5 lb (~2.5 kg) on bench** (same for hinge/leg press variants in the bodybuilding program; smallest increment otherwise). Track RPE from day one to learn calibration but don't program by it yet. Stall ladder: miss reps → repeat same load next week; miss two weeks running → **−10%** then climb again; stall again → halve the increments (+5 → +2.5 lb; micro-load 2 lb). Graduation: when most lifts stall even on halved increments (judge by the *strength* days, never the technique day — it stays submaximal forever).
- **Novice Bodybuilding (4-day, lower/upper ×2)**: no technique day; strength work sits alongside hypertrophy work for progressive overload. ~10–12 sets/muscle; 65% of volume at moderate reps/loads. Glute volume runs high (squat + hinge overlap) and calves/hams look low (indirect work) — read the table through the overlap lens.
- **Intermediate Powerlifting (4-day)**: 13–15 sets/bucket, ~69% competition lifts, 70% heavy. Day 1 volume (squat *variant* — front/high/SBS if low-bar — + bench 7–9 @ RPE 7), Day 2 technique (big 3 1–3 @ 80–85% @ RPE 5, "4–6 reps from failure, always"), Day 3 strength (squat + bench 3–5 @ 82.5–87.5% @ RPE 8), Day 4 strength deadlift (3–5 @ RPE 8) + bench-variant volume (6–8) + pulls. Progression: wave loading on every lift (+10 lb weekly on squat/DL waves, +5 lb elsewhere); deload per checklist after each 3-week wave (deload = lowest reps *and* lowest load of the wave, ⅔ sets — 6 sets→4, 3–4 sets→2); **9 weeks without a deload → deload regardless**; widen a rep range (3–5 → 2–6) if failure keeps intruding, and deloads become mandatory every 2 cycles. Stalled exercise: finish the cycle with reduced loads → deload week 4 unconditionally → restart 5–10% lighter → shrink increments. Seasonal tuning: shift rep/% bands up for intensification (2–4 @ 85–90%) or down for accumulation (4–6 @ 80–85%).
- **Intermediate competition prep**: start a 4-week cycle 4 weeks out with strength days at 1–3 reps; week 3 = deload all non-competition lifts; swap Day 4 to squat→bench→deadlift in order, working to a single opener; meet week: 24–48 h out, 2 singles @ RPE 4 on squat/bench + 1 on deadlift, nothing else. (No novice meet prep exists on purpose — compete as an intermediate.)
- **Intermediate Bodybuilding (5-day)**: Days 1–2 strength-focused lower/upper (3–5 @ 82.5–87.5% on main patterns @ RPE 8), then lower/push/pull days; muscle frequency stays 2×; wave loading for compounds, double progression for isolations. Arm/ant-delt/glute volume reads high (indirect work) — expected.
- **Advanced Powerlifting (6-day)**: block-periodized — **accumulation** (~50% competition lifts, 56% heavy sets, accessories emphasized, 54 sets) → **intensification** (75% competition lifts, 81% heavy, accessories pruned, 48 sets). RPE *ranges* replace first-set RPE: %1RM sets the first load, then subsequent sets shift up/down to land inside the range — which also makes progress visible without testing. Deload = repeat week 3 with ⅔ sets, RPE −1, %1RM −5%; every third block without one → deload anyway. Testing options: AMRAP @ 85% (post-accumulation) or a mock meet (post-intensification). Peaking: accumulation → intensification → modified deload ending in opener practice (87.5–92.5% or RPE 7.5–8.5) one week out → the Level 3 meet-week taper.
- **Advanced Bodybuilding (6-day, lower/upper ×3)**: volume ~16–24 sets/muscle (pecs 17, back 17, triceps 22, glutes 24); accumulation vs intensification blocks keep sets constant and shift rep ranges down/heavier (don't add sets to compensate — heavy + more sets = excessive fatigue). Optional testing week every 8–16 weeks; progress is otherwise read off loads-at-RPE.
- **Accessory menu rules**: rows must spare the lumbar (cable, chest-supported, seal row); pulls = weighted chins if rep/RPE targets allow, else pulldowns; presses on barbell when %1RM-programmed (micro-loading, accurate e1RM); squat/deadlift variants chosen pain-free and fault-suited (Level 4 logic); hinge variants (GHR, back extension, hip thrust) exist to spare the lumbar; 6+-rep "deadlift variants" → RDL or good morning (eccentric self-limits); sumo for bodybuilders = slightly wider than hands, not ultra-wide; close grip = no narrower than a tucked push-up; single-leg work for balance/coordination; "Leg Curl 1/2" = two different variations; grip = near-lockout rack holds 10–20 s @ 90–110% or one-arm hangs; no shrugs or direct ab work needed on compound-heavy programs (add only for genuine weak points); substitutions must preserve movement pattern and overlap accounting.

## Key Concepts
- **"First Set RPE" convention**: novices/intermediates program the load so *set 1* hits the target RPE; all remaining sets ride the same load. Missing later reps = started too heavy, under-rested, or a technical fault — diagnostic information, not failure.
- **Monotony is a risk factor**: programs vary load/reps across days even at novice level because low-variation training is linked to worse performance, overtraining signs, and illness; restoring variation restores performance.
- **Percentages are references, RPE is the truth**: "%1RM is listed to put you roughly at the first-set RPE — if not, adjust." At advanced level, RPE ranges invert the hierarchy entirely.

## Anti-patterns
- Treating a sample program as "the" program — they can't be optimal because they aren't built from *your* adaptation history.
- Waiting for the Day-2 technique day to stall before graduating to the next progression level — judge by strength days.
- Testing maxes on isolation/machine work, or true-failure squats/deadlifts as a novice (use technical failure with solid form, always).
- Adding sets in the intensification block to offset heavier loads.
- High-rep competition deadlifts/squats while peaking for a bodybuilding show — unadapted fatigue; use isolations or leg press instead.

## Worked Example
**Novice powerlifting Day 1, run end-to-end.** Squat 1RM 180 lb → load 70% ≈ 125 lb, 3×8, resisting the urge to do more (it should feel easy; linear increases come weekly). Bench 1RM 200 lb → 140 lb, 3×8. Vertical pull: no percentage — pick a load you'd guess gives ~12 reps, do 10 @ RPE 8, repeat ×4. Log RPE on the %1RM lifts too (calibration). Next week: +10 lb squat, +5 lb bench, next increment on the pull. Week after: same day-to-day comparisons. First missed rep day → repeat; second consecutive → −10% and rebuild.

## Key Takeaways
1. Pick the program by *rate of progress*, not ego: if most lifts stall at halved increments (novice) or 2.5 kg/cycle (intermediate), move up a level.
2. Each day progresses independently — compare Day 1 to last week's Day 1.
3. Technique/power days never stall because they never approach failure; they exist to practice, not to test.
4. Deload math is mechanical: lowest load/reps of the wave at ⅔ the sets (or week 3 repeated at ⅔ sets, RPE −1, load −5%).
5. Dual athletes don't need two programs — they need the right *variant choices and tapers* bolted onto the primary-sport program, switching emphasis 3–6 months before the secondary competition.
