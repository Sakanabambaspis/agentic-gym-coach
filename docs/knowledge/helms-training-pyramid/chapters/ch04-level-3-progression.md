# Level 3: Progression

## Core Idea
Progressive overload is largely handled by correct VIF (Level 2); **progression** is the plan for how overload keeps being applied as you approach your genetic limit — and the closer you are to that limit, the slower progress comes and the more intentional the plan must be. Classify yourself by **rate of improvement** (not strength standards or years lifting): novices progress workout-to-workout, intermediates week-to-week, advanced month-to-month.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Novice — single (linear) progression**: add 5 lb per session (10 lb on squat/deadlift) at the same sets/reps. Miss the rep target → repeat the same load next session. Miss in **two consecutive sessions → reduce load 10%**, same reps/sets, then return to the previous failing load. If stalling across multiple movements even with good sleep/surplus/protein, you're an intermediate now.
- **Intermediate compound — linear-periodized wave**: pick a load for 3×8 clean of failure; each week add 5–10 lb and drop 1 rep (8→7→6); week 4 = deload or repeat heavier. Hypertrophy variant: 12s→10s→8s (drop 2 reps/week, +5 lb). Works because volume-load climbs wave over wave as strength rises.
- **Intermediate isolation — double progression**: can't add 5 lb/session to a curl (5 lb = 10% of a 50 lb curl vs 1.5% of a 355 lb squat). Add **reps** until the top of the range (3×12→3×15), *then* add load and rebuild. Deload week = bottom of range, 2 sets (12,12). If the next dumbbell is too big a jump, widen the range (8–12 → 8–15). Any lift with oversized load jumps (small lifters + dumbbells) is a candidate.
- **Advanced — planned, tested progression**: progress is invisible week-to-week; test 1RM/AMRAP every **6–12 weeks** after a taper, or skip formal testing entirely by tracking RPE (a single @ 7 RPE = your e4RM; triples @ 8–9 RPE ≈ 4–5RM). Estimate 1RM only from ~5RM-or-heavier performances (higher reps are inaccurate). Bodybuilders: track loads-at-RPE trends, not mirror/tape/calipers — even DXA's ~±5% error exceeds a realistic year of advanced gains (~9 lb muscle).
- **Reactive deload decision (post-block checklist)** — yes to **2+** → deload week: dreading the gym? sleep worse than normal? loads/reps decreasing? life stress worse? aches/pains worse? Yes to **0–1** → start the next mesocycle. Yes *only* to aches → "normal" volume week but all sets in 12–20 reps (or BFR on single-joint work). Hard floor: **3 consecutive mesocycles without a deload → deload regardless**.
- **Volume adjustment rules**: chronically under-recovered even after deloads → cut ~20% of sets across the board (15→12). Plateaued while recovering → add **1–2 sets** per muscle/movement (~10% of a 10–20 set range); stalled on one lift → add sets to that lift specifically; strength athlete stalled → consider more muscle (accessory volume, or a weight class up). Check technique first — it's often the real bottleneck.
- **Intro cycle vs deload**: a deload dumps fatigue *after* a block (before an intensity block); an intro week acclimates you *before* a volume block — run ~75% of planned volume at ~1 RPE lower. Pattern: Volume block → deload → Intensity block; Intensity block → intro week → Volume block.
- **Periodization models (integrate, don't choose)**:
  - **Linear (Western)**: volume down, intensity up over months; phases get more sport-specific approaching competition (a powerlifter never peaks with jump squats — end on heavy, low-volume specificity).
  - **Block**: same logic in ≤6-week mesocycles (accumulation → intensification → realization) so adaptations don't decay and you can peak multiple times a season.
  - **Undulating (DUP/WUP)**: rep range/goal rotates by day (DUP) or week (WUP) — hypertrophy/strength/power all trained within the week.
  - "Which model is best?" is the wrong question — nearly every real program is linear over time, undulates within the week, and has block-like phases (5/3/1 for powerlifting is all three at once).
- **DUP for powerlifters — HPS beats HSP** (Zourdos): Day 1 Hypertrophy (6–12 @ RPE 5–8, high volume) → Day 2 Power/technique (1–3 @ 75–90%, RPE 5–7, ≤3–5 sets) → Day 3 Strength (1–6 @ RPE 7–9). Putting the low-fatigue power day between hypertrophy and strength days let lifters do more volume on strength-day AMRAPs and gain more strength.

## Key Concepts
- **Deload prescriptions**: novice = 10% load cut after two failed sessions; post-novice = ~half volume at similar loads, ~2 RPE lower (3×10×200 → 2×8×200).
- **Accumulation block (~6 wk)**: 12→22 sets/muscle/week (+2 sets/week; frequency 2→3×/week in week 4 to accommodate), RPE 5–8, load rises only when easy. Strength: ⅔ in 4–6 reps, ⅓ in 8–12. Hypertrophy: ⅔ in 6–12, ⅓ in 12–15.
- **Intensification block (~4 wk)**: sets fall 22→18→15→12 while load/RPE climb weekly to 9–9.5+ by the end. Strength: ⅔ in 2–5 reps, ⅓ in 6–10. Hypertrophy: ⅔ in 6–10, ⅓ in 3–5.
- **Realization block (~2 wk)**: 10 sets/muscle, same rep ranges at RPE 7–9, accessories halved. Strength: singles at descending RPE (7→6→5 across the week) then 1RM test; hypertrophy: one AMRAP per main lift spread over the week.
- **BFR**: wraps at ~7/10 tightness (no tingling, no color change), 20–30% 1RM sets to failure — equal hypertrophy to heavy training for single-joint work when joints hurt; not a strength tool.

## Anti-patterns
- **Adding volume as the first response to a stall** — check sleep, surplus, protein, RPE honesty, frequency, and technique first.
- **Treating periodization models as mutually exclusive**, or running an advanced structure as a novice (periodization model makes no difference for novices).
- **Testing 1RM frequently**, or estimating it from high-rep AMRAPs.
- **Peaking with non-specific work**: a powerlifter doesn't conclude a cycle at 40% 1RM jump squats.
- **Greedy meet-day attempt selection**: build the biggest *total* — open ~3RM, second ~2RM, take the 2.5 kg incremental PR third. 2.5 kg × 3 lifts × 2–3 meets/year = 15–22.5 kg/year on your total.

## Worked Example
**The 3-phase integrated program (load × sets × reps):**

| Week | Day 1 | Day 2 | Day 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wk1 (Vol) | 200×3×14 | 210×3×9 | 225×3×6 |
| Wk2 | 205×3×12 | 215×3×8 | 230×3×5 |
| Wk3 | 210×3×10 | 220×3×7 | 235×3×4 |
| Wk4 (deload) | 200×2×10 | 210×2×7 | 225×2×4 |
| Wk5–7 (Intensify) | 8–12 reps falling | 6–8 falling | 3–5 falling, loads up |
| Wk8 (Taper+Test) | 210×2×8 | 220×2×6 | 250×AMRAP |

One program, linear over the phase (volume down, intensity up), DUP within each week (three rep ranges), and block-periodized (volume phase → intensity phase → taper/test). Week 4 deload assesses whether week 5 restarts heavier.

**Taper for a powerlifting meet (2 weeks out):** train all three lifts in order some days, 1–3 reps at your 4–6-rep percentages (75–85%) → cut main-lift weekly volume 50–70% → halve accessory sets → opener practice (singles @ RPE 7.5–8.5) 7 days out → meet week: last session 1–2 days out is a primer — single on the big 3 @ RPE 4–5, nothing else.

**Bodybuilder peak week is different**: training exists to help the carb load — keep exercises/split/sets, shift everything to 8–20 reps @ RPE 6–8.5, avoid soreness-inducing movements (heavy eccentrics at long lengths, e.g. RDLs), finish the split 2–3 days out, optional 30-min pump-up @ ≤6 RPE the last 1–2 days.

## Key Takeaways
1. Pick the simplest progression that still works: linear → double/wave → periodized blocks. Upgrade only when the current one stops working.
2. Deloads are a planned, checklisted tool (2+ flags = deload; 3 mesos = mandatory), not an admission of failure.
3. Add volume in ~10% steps (1–2 sets), cut it ~20% when chronically fatigued; fix technique before adding anything.
4. Advanced lifters live by RPE tracking and rare, taper-fed tests — not weekly max-outs.
5. Integrate periodization models; run DUP as HPS (power day between hypertrophy and strength), not HSP.
