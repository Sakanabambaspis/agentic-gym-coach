## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### 1. The Pyramid — order of operations (audit bottom-up, always)
**Adherence → Volume/Intensity/Frequency → Progression → Exercise Selection → Rest Periods → Tempo.** ~80% of results come from the bottom 3–4 levels; the top levels exist to prevent mistakes, not unlock gains. *Periodization is not a level* — it is the time dimension manipulating all levels. When advice conflicts, ask "how important is this relative to my goal, and how does it affect the other variables?" — never "which is universally best?"

### 2. Adherence: REF
Training must be **Realistic** (match time frame and weekly schedule to actual life — optimal ≠ sustainable), **Enjoyable** (enjoyment→effort→results→enjoyment cycle; process-focus beats goal-focus), **Flexible** (autoregulate: run the easy session on a bad day; life stress and training stress share one recovery budget). Missed workout → pick up where you left off; near a competition → skip low-stress sessions or rejig. Back-to-back hard days are fine — drop load to keep intended RPE.

### 3. VIF starting prescription (adjust by response)
- **Volume: 10–20 hard sets per muscle/movement per week** — novice 10–12, intermediate 13–15, advanced 16–20. Count **sets in an intensity zone**, not volume-load or reps. Primary and secondary muscle contributions count 1:1; verify direct work exists (see overlap table in ch03).
- **Intensity (5–10 RPE)**: strength = ⅔–¾ of sets in 1–6 reps, rest 6–15; hypertrophy = ⅔–¾ in 6–12, rest split 1–6 and 12–20. 6–12 is convenient, not magic; floor ~30% 1RM; RPE pairs with every load prescription.
- **Frequency: 2+×/week per muscle/movement**, usually 3–5 sessions/week; frequency *organizes* volume — more volume needs more sessions.
- RPE scale: 10 = failure, 9 = 1 RIR, 8 = 2 RIR, 7 = 3 RIR, 5–6 = 4–6 RIR. %1RM→reps averages: 100→1, 90→3–4, 85→5–6, 80→7–8, 75→9–10, 70→11–12 (wide individual spread — hence RPE).
- Failure: a tool with a purpose (isolation, last sets, testing) — never on big compounds.

### 4. Fitness-Fatigue Model & the deload doctrine
Performance = **fitness − fatigue**; fatigue masks fitness and dissipates faster. Volume dose-response is U-shaped — moderate beat high in the landmark studies. **Do enough volume to progress; increase only when plateaued *and* recovering** (+1–2 sets ≈ +10%; if chronically under-recovered, cut ~20%). Functional overreaching + deload = PRs; pushing through = nonfunctional overreaching (wheel-spinning); true overtraining is rare. **Reactive deload checklist** after each mesocycle: dreading gym / sleep worse / loads-reps falling / stress worse / aches worse — **2+ yes = deload** (only-aches = 12–20-rep or BFR week); mandatory by the 3rd mesocycle. Recipes: novice −10% load after 2 failed sessions; post-novice ½ volume −2 RPE; wave → lowest wave load/reps @ ⅔ sets; block → repeat wk3 @ ⅔ sets, −1 RPE, −5%.

### 5. Progression by training age (classified by *rate of progress*)
Novice (progress workout-to-workout): **linear load** — +5/+10 lb per session; miss → repeat; 2 misses → −10%; halved increments stall on most lifts → graduate. Intermediate (week-to-week): **wave loading** for compounds (8→7→6, +load weekly), **double progression** for isolation (reps to top of range, then load). Advanced (month-to-month): **block periodization** — accumulation (sets 12→22, RPE 5–8, ~6 wk) → intensification (sets →12, RPE →9–10, ~4 wk) → realization/taper (10 sets, RPE 7–9) → test every 6–12 weeks (e1RM only from ~5RM-heavier; RPE tracking can replace testing). Progression ≠ progressive overload — overload comes from VIF; progression is the career-long plan.

### 6. Specificity & exercise selection
Strength: **competition lifts = 50–75% of volume**; accessories fix weak links. Hypertrophy: **1–2 compounds + 1–3 isolations per muscle group**; compounds stay static across mesocycles, isolations rotate (proficiency gates growth — "muscle confusion" delays overload). Weak-point triage: structural → substitute; activation → re-grip/re-cue; weak link → direct accessory attack. Sticking points: never pause *at* the stall (you'd train less force where you're weakest, and the visible stall is past the true deficit) — use motor-learning pauses, fault-punishing variants, isometrics, or individualized speed work. Compounds first in the session; full ROM you actually own; MMC only for isolation/technique learning.

### 7. Rest & tempo — don't get them wrong
Rest until ready (**≥1.5 min** isolation, **≥2.5 min** compounds); short rests have never beaten long for growth. Save time with **antagonist paired sets** (opposite muscles; ~2 min compound pairs, ~1 min isolation; banned around squat/deadlift/lunge). Drop sets/rest-pause = accessory-only time-savers. Tempo: **controlled eccentric, maximal-intent concentric**; reps ≥10 s harm growth; think impulse (force × time), not time under tension. Supramaximal eccentrics: unproven, high-cost.

### 8. Autoregulation & special contexts
Float training days (2–3-day weeks) or off days (4+); pair %1RM with an RPE range; rotate exercises within proficiency limits (main-lift variants meso-to-meso far from competition). **Cutting**: mild = nothing changes; aggressive = drop one volume tier ~⅓ in, deload automatically every meso, trust RPE. **Taper**: cut volume ⅓–⅔, keep intensity; opener practice @ RPE 7.5–8.5 a week out; primer singles @ RPE 4–5 24–48 h out. Attempt selection: opener ≈ 3RM, second ≈ 2RM, third = 2.5 kg PR. Bodybuilder peak week serves the carb load (8–20 reps @ RPE 6–8.5).

For hands-on coaching or medical/rehab decisions, combine with qualified professional input — the authors explicitly defer injury treatment to sports-medicine specialists. Percentages and set counts are starting guidelines to adjust by individual response, not laws.
